# Chapter 1 — Our Identity

## Who we are

AfriMarket — *Twenzetu Sokoni* — is an African enterprise platform for commerce. We build the trusted marketplace infrastructure that connects buyers, vendors, and service providers across communities, starting in Tanzania and scaling across the continent.

We are not a feature. We are a platform: a set of Enterprise Suites that work together under one constitution, one identity system, and one set of engineering standards (see [Enterprise Suites](../enterprise-suites.md)).

## Our story

- AfriMarket is born in Tanzania, for Africa.
- We launched as a live multi-tenant marketplace at `twenzetusokoni.com` — real vendors, real products, real orders, real people.
- We are engineered to global standards from day one: secure by default, observable, multi-tenant, mobile-first, and available in Kiswahili and English.
- We reach users wherever they are: web, Android (Trusted Web Activity), and USSD — because commerce in Africa happens beyond the smartphone screen.

## Identity statements

- **We are African.** Our solutions are designed from African context outward — not adapted inward. Language, payments, mobile money, logistics, and trust all start with the user in their community.
- **We are builders.** We ship working software. The platform is live and used, not a slide deck.
- **We are a platform.** We grow by composing capabilities into Enterprise Suites — Marketplace, Identity, Payments, Analytics, and beyond — each measurable and accountable.
- **We are trustworthy.** Trust is our most valuable asset. Vendors, buyers, and partners must be able to rely on AfriMarket with their money, their data, and their reputation.

## What we are not

- We are not a single-purpose app that we abandon once shipped.
- We are not a walled garden; open standards and documented APIs guide our growth.
- We are not a platform that treats users as data; privacy is a principle, not a compliance checkbox.

## Tagline

> **Twenzetu Sokoni** — Let us go to our market.

## Implications for the platform

Every repository, service, and decision should be able to answer: *"Does this make the market more accessible, more trusted, and more capable for the people who depend on it?"* If the answer is not yes, it does not belong in AfriMarket.
