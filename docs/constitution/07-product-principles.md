# Chapter 7 — Product Principles

Product principles govern what we build for our users and how we decide what matters.

## 7.1 Solve real problems

- Every feature starts from a real user problem, in our real markets — not from a competitor's feature list.
- A feature without a named user or partner who needs it is a hypothesis, not a roadmap item.
- Transport requests, order tracking, and vendor listings exist because real vendors and buyers needed them.

## 7.2 Local-first, world-class execution

- Products are designed from African context outward:
  - Kiswahili and English throughout,
  - mobile money (M-Pesa, AzamPay) as a first-class payment method,
  - USSD for feature phones — the market is bigger than the app store,
  - low-bandwidth-friendly web experiences.
- Context is local; quality is global.

## 7.3 Trust is a product feature

- Buyers and vendors must understand their money and their data at all times.
- Clear order states, transparent fares, receipts, and audit trails are product requirements.
- We never use dark patterns to nudge behavior.

## 7.4 Privacy by design

- We collect only what we need, keep it only as long as needed, and expose it only with consent.
- Users control their data; privacy documentation is part of the product (see `twenzetusokoni.com/privacy`).

## 7.5 Accessibility and inclusion

- The platform works for people with limited data, limited devices, limited literacy, and limited connectivity.
- Mobile-first web, readable type, simple flows, and USSD as the great equalizer.

## 7.6 Measure what matters

- Success metrics are defined before the feature is built (activation, completion, retention, trust).
- We ship with instrumentation, and we kill features that do not move a named metric.

## 7.7 Product governance

- Roadmaps are owned, reviewed, and published (see Governance, Chapter 8).
- Every product decision is traceable to a recorded rationale.
- We say no to scope that does not serve the Vision and Mission — politely, but firmly.

## 7.8 Sustainability

- The platform must be financially sustainable to serve its users for decades. Pricing, billing, and cost discipline are product decisions, not accounting footnotes.
- The Finance suite exists to make this true from the beginning.
